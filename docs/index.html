Decision support system — climate + healthcare

Climate-aware hospital resource planning

A two-tier decision support system: a validated annual/regional model for seasonal capacity planning, and a daily scenario simulator for short-term operational readiness — built on real French climate and emergency department data.

The problem

Hospitals largely respond to heatwave-driven surges in emergency department demand after they hit, despite heat being forecastable days in advance. No standardized mechanism translates climate data into a hospital capacity decision.

How it works — two timescales

Annual model for seasonal strategy, daily simulator for operational readiness. Neither replaces the other.

REAL DATA

Annual / Regional Model

  • 13 French regions × 2016-2024, real Santé publique France + Eurostat data
  • Random Forest, validated with leave-one-year-out and leave-one-region-out cross-validation
  • Outputs a seasonal risk tier and capacity-increase percentage per region
REAL + SIMULATED

Daily Scenario Simulator

  • Real daily temperature (2018-2023, 95 departments) aggregated to 13 regions
  • Validated event-study finding: compound heat days show the strongest ED demand signal
  • Future scenarios sampled from real historical distributions; daily capacity simulated and calibrated to real annual baseline (no public daily bed-occupancy data exists in France)

What we found — the honest version

Not every approach worked. Here's what did, and what didn't, and why that matters.

TESTED

Continuous regression

Daily temperature → daily ED visits, as a straight-line relationship. Failed (negative R²) — heat's effect isn't linear.

FOUND

Threshold effect

Comparing heatwave days vs. normal days directly: statistically significant, p<0.00001, real lag effect confirmed.

STRENGTHENED

Compound heat

Adding overnight temperature (TMin): compound hot-day-plus-warm-night is the strongest signal found, +2.33%.

SCOPED

Known limitations

Diagnosis-specific ED data (RPU) is institutionally restricted in France — documented, not hidden.

Heat Analysis — what the data shows

Real event-study findings, 2018-2023, 95 French departments.

REAL DATA

The lag effect

  • Hot days: +1.43% ED visits above normal (p<0.00001)
  • Effect peaks 1 day after the hot day (+2.00%), not same-day
  • Independently confirms the lagged heat-health relationship documented in Flower et al. (2026, BMJ Open)
REAL DATA

Compound heat (day + night)

  • Hot day alone: +1.84% · Warm night alone: +1.79%
  • Both together: +2.33% — the strongest signal found
  • Mechanism: warm nights remove the body's overnight recovery window
REAL DATA

Department sensitivity ranking

Ranking each department against its OWN hot vs. normal days reveals sensitivity isn't just about absolute heat. Corsica ranks highest, as expected — but Ardennes and Meuse (cooler, northern departments) also rank in the top 5, likely because less-acclimatized populations and lower AC prevalence make even a locally "hot" day more disruptive. Caveat: each department has ~220 hot-day observations (vs. ~20,600 pooled nationally), so individual rankings are directional, not as statistically strong as the headline finding.

Hospital DSS — the recommendation, not just a number

Every scenario run produces three concrete, actionable outputs — not a description of current capacity.

+N beds
Extra beds recommended on compound heat days
Scaled from the real +2.33% demand signal against the region's real bed baseline
+N nurses
Extra nursing staff recommended
Documented ratio assumption — no public daily staffing data exists in France
+N doctors
Extra physician staff recommended
Same documented ratio approach, applied consistently

The annual model's capacity-gap logic expresses its recommendation as a percentage capacity increase (e.g. "+10% above baseline") — kept as a percentage deliberately, since it scales cleanly across regions of very different sizes and is the right unit for seasonal, strategic planning. The daily scenario tab converts this into concrete counts for a specific short-term window, since that's what an administrator needs to actually schedule.
